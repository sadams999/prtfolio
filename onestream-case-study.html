> the problem

Behind the English site

Localized pages are easy to leave behind. Here they were also missing the structured answers people now expect AI assistants to cite.

The Spanish and French pages used an older header and footer style, applied color and typography inconsistently against the brand guidelines, and had little question-and-answer content. Project Clarity, the Sanity content release, was the chance to fix all of it at once.

> what i shipped

Three updates, two languages

Every change was made inside Sanity's content structure so the pages stay accurate and in sync with the English site.

FAQs in Spanish and French

Question-and-answer sections written so each answer stands on its own and can be found, and cited, by search and LLMs.

Headers and footers

Updated to the Project Clarity style so navigation matches the rest of the site.

Brand alignment

Color usage and typography updated to follow the brand guidelines across the translated pages.

Video resource pages

Moved video resource pages to a new, updated template with more surrounding context and an added transcript for each video, for better LLM discoverability.

> approach

How I worked

Three decisions shaped the work: how to write the FAQs, how to apply the brand, and how to build it in Sanity.

FAQs built for people and LLMs

Each answer leads with the direct answer, uses the wording someone would type into a search box or assistant, and covers one topic per question. That makes it quick to scan and easy for a model to quote accurately.

Brand color and typography

I applied the guidelines to the localized templates so a Spanish or French visitor sees the same brand as an English one. Both languages run longer than English, so headings, buttons and navigation needed room to grow.

Working within Sanity

Because the pages live in Sanity, I worked within its content structure so the updates are maintainable content, not one-off page edits.
